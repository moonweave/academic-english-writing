# Materials: five core patterns

Editorial analyses inspected 2026-10-23. Short quotations identify source wording; ellipses are not used to reconstruct long passages. Grammatical analyses and abstract schemata are editorial interpretations. Every application sentence below is an original invented teaching example, not a source finding or a reusable data claim. Choose a pattern because it serves the argument; do not insert every pattern or copy a source’s distinctive wording.

## M1 — core source

Jeong-Yun Sun et al. (2012), “Highly stretchable and tough hydrogels,” Nature 489, 133–136. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11409); [publisher PDF hosted by authors](https://suo.seas.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum4271/files/suo/files/273.pdf). Full text available; named passages inspected.

### M1a: Lead with the limitation, emphasize the result

**Locator:** p. 133, opening paragraph. **Source fragment:** “they can be stretched beyond 20 times their initial length”.

**Structure (editorial schematic, not a quotation):** Quoted source clause: [subject] + [can be + past participle] + [extent complement]. In context: [Although + subject + verb + property], [main assertion]. The main assertion receives the emphasis.

**How it works:** Concessive subordinate clause → main assertion. The present-tense concession establishes an unfavorable property; the main clause receives the informative stress. Keep the qualification subordinate only if the result is the intended focus.

**Original application:** Although the coating remained porous, its water uptake decreased from 18% to 11% after treatment.

**Limits:** This example assumes both porosity and uptake were measured. Do not manufacture a tension or imply a causal link between them.

### M1b: Give interacting components explicit roles

**Locator:** p. 136, notched hybrid-gel mechanism paragraph. **Source fragment:** “the polyacrylamide network bridges the crack and stabilizes deformation”.

**Structure (editorial schematic, not a quotation):** [component subject] + [active verb + object] + and + [active verb + object]. Both predicates share the named subject; no hidden actor is needed.

**How it works:** A concrete network subject governs coordinated active verbs. The next sentence can pick up the process rather than introduce an unexplained “this.” Present tense expresses the proposed explanatory model, not necessarily a universal fact.

**Original application:** The rigid phase carries the load, while the soft phase accommodates deformation. This division of roles limits strain localization.

**Limits:** Use the second sentence only with evidence for localization. An active verb cannot turn a proposed mechanism into a demonstrated cause.

## M2 — core source

K. S. Novoselov et al. (2004), “Electric Field Effect in Atomically Thin Carbon Films,” Science 306, 666–669. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1102896); [author preprint v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0410550v1). Analysis applies to this preprint, not a verified word-for-word publisher edition.

### M2a: Expose the physical obstacle

**Locator:** preprint p. 1, first main paragraph. **Source fragment:** “However, this would require atomically thin metal films”.

**Structure (editorial schematic, not a quotation):** [However], [anaphoric subject] + [would require] + [required object], because [physical reason]. The quote is the requirement clause; the schematic summarizes the adjacent explanation.

**How it works:** Contrast marker → conditional-modal requirement → because-clause giving the obstacle. Would marks what the proposed route would require; it does not describe an experiment already performed.

**Original application:** Reducing the electrode spacing would shorten the transport path, but it would also require insulation that remains intact during bending.

**Limits:** The requirement must follow from the actual design. Do not borrow a historical novelty claim or use would where the experiment was completed.

### M2b: Introduce a model after its observables

**Locator:** preprint p. 2, Fig. 2 discussion. **Source fragment:** “Our measurements can be explained quantitatively by a model”.

**Structure (editorial schematic, not a quotation):** [observations] -> [measurement subject] + [can be explained] + [by a model]. [Relation] -> [immediate symbol definitions]. This is an explanatory claim, not proof of uniqueness.

**How it works:** Observation sentences precede a model sentence; the modal passive can be explained identifies explanatory adequacy. Define symbols immediately after the governing relation. This order gives the reader an observable to interpret before the abstraction.

**Original application:** The resistance decreased as pressure increased. A contact-area model reproduces this trend, with A denoting the effective contact area.

**Limits:** Reproduces requires a checked model result; visual resemblance alone supports weaker wording. Do not imply uniqueness or fit parameters absent from the supplied evidence.


## M3 — core source, abstract only

Jian Zhao et al. (2024), “Ultrahigh-mobility semiconducting epitaxial graphene on silicon carbide,” Nature 625, 60–65. [DOI/publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06811-0). Abstract inspected 2026-10-23; subscription body not inspected. Provisional editorial selection; attention evidence is in [the selection record](selection-rationale.md).

### M3a: Define the material state at its first operational use

**Locator:** abstract, sentence introducing the quasi-equilibrium annealing method. **Source fragment:** “that is, a well-ordered buffer layer”.

**Structure (editorial schematic, not a quotation):** [method produces + material label] + [(that is, + defining noun phrase)] + [location]. The quoted phrase is an appositive definition, not a finite clause.

**How it works:** The definition links the new label to an already introduced physical state, before the next sentence reuses the label. Keep the explanatory parenthesis short enough that the main predicate remains readable.

**Original application:** Annealing produced the ordered interphase (that is, the aligned surface layer) along the electrode edge.

**Limits:** The example is invented. Use “that is” only for equivalence; use “for example” for a member of a class. Defining a label does not establish composition or order without characterization. This abstract cannot supply a Results-section model.
