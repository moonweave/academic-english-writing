# Materials: four patterns from two papers

Editorial analyses, inspected 2026-10-23. Full text available; named passages closely inspected. No source sentences copied. These entries select useful local decisions, not every expression in a paper.

## M1 — Sun et al. (2012)

Source: Jeong-Yun Sun et al., “Highly stretchable and tough hydrogels,” Nature 489, 133–136. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11409); [author-hosted publisher PDF](https://suo.seas.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum4271/files/suo/files/273.pdf).

**M1a: Quantify the obstacle before the contribution.** Location: p. 133, opening paragraph. The text narrows from applications to mechanical limitations, introduces numerical benchmarks, and then reports the new material. Contrastive and concessive clauses keep competing properties visible. Use when the benchmark measures the same property under relevant conditions; never replace comparable testing with an impressive list of numbers.

**M1b: Explain an interaction through concrete subjects.** Location: p. 136, paragraph describing a notched hybrid gel and the two networks. The network components become grammatical subjects, and verbs specify complementary physical roles. The paragraph connects mechanism steps instead of repeating that performance improved. Use only when the evidence supports those roles; a convenient subject–verb structure does not establish causation. Avoid inheriting evaluative adjectives by default.

## M2 — Novoselov et al. (2004)

Source: K. S. Novoselov et al., “Electric Field Effect in Atomically Thin Carbon Films,” Science 306, 666–669. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1102896); [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0410550). Analysis applies to this preprint, not a word-for-word verified publisher edition.

**M2a: Make the obstacle physically specific.** Location: preprint p. 1, first main paragraph. The problem narrows from voltage control to screening, required thickness, and film instability. Causal and conditional clauses explain why the obvious approach is difficult. Use when each premise is supported; do not import historical novelty claims into current prose.

**M2b: Report first, model second.** Location: preprint p. 2, Fig. 2 discussion. Observable trends precede a quantitative model and variable definitions. Present tense describes the figure/model; observation and explanation remain distinguishable. Use with an actual model and defined variables; a fitted explanation is not automatically unique proof. Preprint typos and exuberant emphasis are not style targets.
