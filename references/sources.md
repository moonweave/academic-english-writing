# Source provenance

Inspection: 2026-10-23. This is a compact guidance library, not a trained model or comprehensive corpus of excellent papers.

## Writing guidance

- George D. Gopen and Judith A. Swan, “The Science of Scientific Writing,” *American Scientist* 78(6), 550–558 (1990). [Article hosted by UCL](https://www.gatsby.ucl.ac.uk/~pel/misc/gopen_swan.pdf). Access: article text, including subject–verb separation, topic/stress positions, and contextual connections. Used for reader-oriented information-order heuristics, not a claim that one syntax is always superior.
- John Morley / University of Manchester, [Academic Phrasebank](https://www.phrasebank.manchester.ac.uk/) and [About](https://www.phrasebank.manchester.ac.uk/about-academic-phrasebank/). Access: descriptions and indexed text. Used for organizing language by communicative function and adapting generic patterns to context.
- University of Manchester, [Being cautious](https://www.phrasebank.manchester.ac.uk/using-cautious-language/). Access: indexed page text. Used for calibrating uncertain explanations and generalizations, not hedging direct observations.

Domain checks, original examples, and workflow are editorial synthesis, not source quotations or findings. Grammar heuristics rely on ordinary English usage; check current journal guidance when journal-specific conventions matter.
