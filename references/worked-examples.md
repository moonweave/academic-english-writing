# Original worked examples

All inputs, outputs, and numerical values below are invented teaching examples. They are not quotations, paraphrases of a particular paper, or research evidence. Their purpose is to demonstrate decisions, not provide interchangeable sentence templates.

## Materials: replace an evaluation with its comparison

Input facts: annealed film conductivity 3 S/m; untreated control 2 S/m; both measured at 25 °C; no statistical analysis supplied.

Weak draft: “The annealed film demonstrated outstanding electrical properties, highlighting the remarkable effectiveness of the treatment.”

Revision: “At 25 °C, the annealed film had a conductivity of 3 S/m, compared with 2 S/m for the untreated control.”

Decision: specify quantity, comparator, and common condition. No invented significance or causal mechanism.

## Polymers: separate the measurement from a possible explanation

Input: “팽윤비가 감소했다. 가교 밀도가 증가했을 가능성이 있다. 가교 밀도를 직접 측정하지 않았다.”

Revision: “The swelling ratio decreased, which is consistent with an increase in crosslink density. Crosslink density was not measured directly.”

Decision: report the observation directly and qualify the interpretation locally. Do not imply that swelling uniquely determines crosslink density.

## Results: make the next experiment follow from the uncertainty

Input facts: storage modulus decreased during prolonged heating; geometry-related signal loss possible; a complementary measurement reproduced the trend; late-stage discrepancy remained.

Revision: “The storage modulus decreased during prolonged heating. To assess whether changes in sample geometry contributed to this decrease, we repeated the measurement using the complementary protocol. The decreasing trend persisted, although the late-stage values differed between protocols.”

Decision: observation → unresolved alternative → purpose of check → bounded result. No assertion that the alternative was completely eliminated.

## Electronics: preserve simulation and accounting boundaries

Input facts: simulated ADC; supply 1.2 V; conversion rate 10 MS/s; core power 2 mW; peripherals excluded.

Revision: “In simulation, the ADC core consumed 2 mW from a 1.2 V supply at 10 MS/s. This estimate excludes peripheral circuitry.”

Decision: retain evidence type and power boundary. Do not turn core power into total-chip power or measured silicon performance.

## Grammar: choose article and tense by reference

Input: “We fabricated transistor. Transistor exhibits hysteresis during yesterday measurement.”

Revision: “We fabricated a transistor. The transistor exhibited hysteresis during yesterday's measurement.”

Decision: introduce an object before using the definite article; simple past reports a completed, dated observation. Present tense is still appropriate elsewhere for what a figure shows.

## Paragraph: narrow a broad opening without inventing novelty

Input facts: polymer film used in repeated deformation; response drifts; cause uncertain; study varies rest time at fixed strain amplitude. No outcome supplied.

Weak draft: “Flexible materials have revolutionized modern technology. However, many challenges remain. Here, we present a groundbreaking investigation into this important issue.”

Revision: “Polymer films used under repeated deformation can exhibit response drift, complicating comparisons across loading cycles. The contribution of recovery between cycles remains uncertain in the present system. We therefore varied the rest time while holding the strain amplitude constant.”

Decision: connect the actual problem to a discriminating measurement. No claim that the literature is empty and no invented result.
